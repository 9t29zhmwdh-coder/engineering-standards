# Writing Style

Applies to every piece of text produced for this portfolio: commit messages, PR bodies, READMEs, changelogs, repository descriptions, profile and bio fields, documentation, code comments, and anything generated by an assistant on the maintainer's behalf. The rules are checked by reading the text back before it is written or sent, not by remembering them.

Short, polished text is where this matters most. A single violation in a bio, a tagline or a repository description is the whole text.

## 1. No Dashes as Punctuation

Never use an em-dash (`—`, U+2014), an en-dash (`–`, U+2013), or a hyphen standing in for one. Hyphens inside compound words are unaffected: `Bundle-ID` and `AI-Code-Assistent` are correct, `the fix — which took an hour — landed` is not.

Rewrite the sentence rather than swapping one dash for another. A colon introduces a list or an explanation, a comma carries a subordinate clause, and two sentences are usually clearer than one that needed a dash to hold it together.

Search the text mechanically for `—` and `–` before committing or sending it:

```bash
grep -rn "—\|–" --include="*.md" . --exclude-dir=.git
```

**Why this rule exists:** dashes used this way are a strong stylistic marker of machine-generated text. This portfolio is openly AI-assisted (see [`governance.md`](governance.md) and `CLAUDE.md` section 12), and that transparency only works when the writing itself reads as deliberate rather than generated. A violation in the GitHub bio cost real time to notice and fix on 2026-07-10.

**Existing text is not a precedent.** A scan across 30 repositories found 95 violations sitting in old changelog headers and feature descriptions. Their presence means the rule arrived after them, not that they are acceptable. Fix what you touch; do not copy the pattern because it is already there.

## 2. Swiss German Orthography

- Never `ß`, always `ss`: "weiss", "heiss", "Strasse", "grüssen", "dass"
- Umlauts written properly as `ä`, `ö`, `ü`, never transliterated to `ae`, `oe`, `ue`, and the same applies to any other language's diacritics
- Tone is direct and informal: "du", never "Sie"

## 3. German and English in Compounds

When a compound word contains English parts, use `AI`, not `KI`:

- Correct: `AI-File-Organizer`, `AI-Log-Aggregator`, `AI-Chat-Interface`, `AI-Code-Assistent`, `AI-Agent`
- Wrong: `KI-File-Organizer`, `KI-Dateiorganizer`

Purely German nouns keep `KI`: `KI-Lebensplaner`, `KI-Zusammenfassung`, `KI-gestützt`.

Do not translate established English technical terms into German: "Tools" not "Werkzeuge", "Daily Driver", "AI-Lab" not "KI-Labor", "Home-Portal", "System-Health-Monitor", "Foundation-Model-Orchestration".

## 4. First Person in Solo-Maintainer Documents

`SECURITY.md`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` and `PRIVACY.md` are written in the first person singular. An institutional "we" implies a team and a support structure that does not exist behind a single maintainer, which is a small dishonesty in exactly the documents where trust is being asked for.

- Correct: "I aim to respond within 72 hours", "I as the maintainer pledge", "## What I Collect"
- Wrong: "We aim to respond", "## Our Pledge", "## What We Collect"

Title `PRIVACY.md` as `# Privacy Policy: ToolName`, with a colon.

## 5. Bilingual Documents

READMEs exist in English (`README.md`, the default) and German (`README.de.md`), cross-linked at the top. Everything under `standards/` and `templates/` is English, including this file. `CLAUDE.md` is German and normative; `CLAUDE.en.md` is a reference translation that follows its structure.

When one language version changes, the other changes in the same commit. A translation that lags is worse than no translation, because a reader cannot tell which version is current. See [`documentation.md`](documentation.md) section 5.
